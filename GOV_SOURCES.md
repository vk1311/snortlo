# GOV_SOURCES: what politician footage Snortlo can reuse commercially

Research date: 2026-10-08. Every page below was read on that date. Policy dates or versions are given where the page shows one.
Snortlo is a monetized channel on YouTube, Facebook and Instagram, so **every use counts as commercial**. This applies even when the clip is funny, short or credited.

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** This is a research summary prepared by an AI assistant from public web pages. It is not a legal opinion. Policies change. Before relying on any source for revenue, have a Canadian IP or media lawyer check the plan, and read the live policy page again.
> Lines marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed from an official page during this research.

**Bottom line for Canada:** no Canadian legislature or federal government source was found that allows monetized reuse without permission. Snortlo has two clean routes: (1) written permission or a licence from the Speaker or rights holder, or (2) items explicitly published under OGL-Canada or a Creative Commons licence that allows commercial use. Fair dealing for parody or satire is a defence you would argue after a complaint. It is not a permission.

## (b) Per-source notes

### UNITED STATES

#### 0. US House floor (baseline, already confirmed)
- The House's floor records say: "Pursuant to Title 17 Section 105 … not subject to copyright protection and is in the public domain" (confirmed earlier by the caller). Under 17 USC 105(a), copyright "is not available for any work of the United States Government". [Cornell LII 17 USC 105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/105)
- **House rule to note.** House Rule V, clause 2(c), in the 119th Congress pamphlet dated Jan 2025, says floor coverage "including any recording thereof— (1) may not be used for any partisan political campaign purpose; (2) may not be used in any commercial advertisement; and (3) may not be broadcast with commercial sponsorship except as part of a bona fide news program or public affairs documentary program." [House Rules 119th (PDF)](https://rules.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/rules.house.gov/files/documents/houserules119thupdated.pdf); older text: [HMAN-107](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/HMAN-107/html/HMAN-107-pg375.htm)
  - This is a House rule, not copyright law. How it binds the general public, and whether platform ads next to a meme clip count as "commercial sponsorship", is **UNVERIFIED**. Practical reading: never put House footage in an ad or sponsored segment, never use it for a campaign, and present clips as commentary or satire.
- **US works abroad (theoretical).** The House Report on §105 says the public-domain rule "is not intended to have any effect on protection of these works abroad" ([govinfo 17 USC 105 notes](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2020-title17/html/USCODE-2020-title17-chap1-sec105.htm)). In theory the US could assert copyright in Canada. No sign was found that it does this for congressional video, so this is **UNVERIFIED** and practically low risk.

#### 1. US Senate floor video (Senate Recording Studio)
- **Copyright:** Senate staff are US Government employees, so their work falls under 17 USC 105 ([LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/105)). **No senate.gov page was found that expressly says floor video is public domain (UNVERIFIED).** C-SPAN's policy treats federal floor coverage differently from "any event not in the public domain" ([C-SPAN, archived](http://web.archive.org/web/20260917181332/https://www.c-span.org/about/copyrightsAndLicensing/)), which is consistent with public domain.
- **Restriction (political, not commercial):** S.Res.28 (99th Congress), §6, as amended by S.Res.431 on June 7, 1988: "The use of any tape duplication of radio or television coverage of the proceedings of the Senate for political campaign purposes is strictly prohibited." Copies are supplied on a written condition of no campaign use. News organizations are bound too but don't sign. [Senate Manual (govinfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SMAN-113/html/SMAN-113-pg129-3.htm). NARA's agreement form (version 1.8.2014) applies the same rule to floor and committee recordings: [NARA PDF](https://www.archives.gov/files/legislative/research/getting-started/senate-video-agreement.pdf). The Senate Ethics Committee repeated this in a "Dear Colleague" letter dated May 1, 2024: [ethics.senate.gov PDF](https://www.ethics.senate.gov/public/_cache/files/e795e26b-558f-4f62-bfd8-4e972c96b05a/dear-colleague---use-of-video-and-photos-of-official-proceedings-and-events-for-a-campaign-purpose.pdf).
- **No explicit commercial ban was found** for Senate floor video, unlike the House's "commercial advertisement" clause. An old CRS guide notes that Library of Congress buyers signed an agreement not to use copies "for political or commercial purposes" ([CRS 95-728, 1995](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/95-728C.html)). That rule is outdated and applied only to tapes bought from LoC.
- **Where to get it:** senate.gov "Floor Proceedings" has a Floor Webcast you can search back to the 112th Congress, 2nd session, and links to C-SPAN for older video ([senate.gov floor proceedings](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/floor_activity_pail.htm)). **Whether senate.gov offers direct downloads is UNVERIFIED** (the player is embedded). If you download from C-SPAN, check that the clip is the raw government feed and remove any C-SPAN branding, or rely on C-SPAN's licence terms (see #5).

#### 2. House/Senate committee hearing video shot by the committees
- If committee staff record it, it is a federal work and should be public domain under 17 USC 105. **No committee was found with an explicit public-domain notice; check each committee (UNVERIFIED).**
- House Rule XI cl. 4(b): committee audio and video "may not be used for any partisan political campaign purpose or be made available for such use". Cl. 4(c) also sets decorum and "distort[ion]" standards for coverage. [House Rules 119th](https://rules.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/rules.house.gov/files/documents/houserules119thupdated.pdf)
- Senate committee hearings: same no-campaign-use condition ([NARA agreement](https://www.archives.gov/files/legislative/research/getting-started/senate-video-agreement.pdf)).
- **Watch out:** many committee streams carry network or pool feeds, witness exhibits or played-in media, and those keep their own copyright. Hearing footage taken from **C-SPAN** falls under C-SPAN's licence: C-SPAN lists "Congressional committee hearings" as federal events and still bans unlicensed commercial use ([C-SPAN](http://web.archive.org/web/20260917181332/https://www.c-span.org/about/copyrightsAndLicensing/)).

#### 3. White House
- whitehouse.gov Copyright Policy: "Pursuant to federal law, government-produced materials appearing on this site are not copyright protected." Also: "Except where otherwise noted, third-party content on this site is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 License." [whitehouse.gov/copyright](https://www.whitehouse.gov/copyright/) (undated page)
  - What this means: government-shot video is public domain. Third-party items need CC BY attribution, or are marked otherwise (e.g. network or pool footage, licensed music). The official White House YouTube channel is assumed to follow the same split (**UNVERIFIED**; check each video for music or network footage).
- **Seal:** 18 USC 713(a) makes it a crime to display the President's seal, among others, in any "telecast, or other production … in a manner reasonably calculated to convey, a false impression of sponsorship or approval" by the US Government. §713(b) restricts reproducing the seal on articles or merch. [Cornell LII 18 USC 713](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/713). Keep the seal and podium crest out of thumbnails and branding. Incidental appearance in a clip is not the same as using it as a logo.
- **President's likeness:** the White House Counsel has a long-standing practice of objecting to commercial **advertising** that uses the President's likeness (e.g. the 2010 Weatherproof billboard). The only source found is press coverage ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/white-house-pressures-clothing-company-into-removing-obama-billboard.amp)), so this is **UNVERIFIED as a formal policy**. Commentary or satire videos are not ads. Never imply the President or the administration endorses Snortlo or any product.

#### 4. Federal agencies
- **NASA** (page updated Aug 13, 2026): NASA content "generally [is] not subject to copyright in the United States". "If the NASA material is to be used for commercial purposes … it must not explicitly or implicitly convey NASA's endorsement." The insignia, logotype and identifiers "are not in the public domain". For identifiable people, commercial use "may infringe that person's right of privacy or publicity". Currently employed astronauts can't appear in "commercial products, advertisements, promotional material". NASA's insignia must not appear with AI-generated imagery. [NASA images & media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
- **DVIDS / Department of Defense, styled "Department of War"** (DoDI 5410.19 Vol. 1, Sept 29, 2021 as amended; page links updated Sept 15, 2025): work by government employees is "not eligible for copyright protection in the United States", but some items belong to third parties. Footage "may not be used in a manner that could imply endorsement of … any political party or candidate". Commercial users must display: "The appearance of U.S. Department of War (DoW) visual information does not imply or constitute DoW endorsement." Insignia and seals are trademarks. Publicity and privacy rights of the people shown are not waived. [DVIDS copyright](https://www.dvidshub.net/about/copyright)
- **NOAA / National Weather Service:** information is "in the public domain, unless specifically noted otherwise, and may be used without charge for any lawful purpose so long as you do not: 1) claim it is your own … 2) use it in a manner that implies an endorsement or affiliation". It "shall not be modified in content and then presented as official". The logo needs permission. [weather.gov disclaimer](https://www.weather.gov/disclaimer). A general NOAA-wide copyright page was not found (**UNVERIFIED** beyond NWS).
- **General rule for all agencies:** public domain covers only the government's own work. Logos and insignia are protected as trademarks, endorsement can't be implied, identifiable people keep publicity and privacy rights, and third-party content must be checked item by item.

#### 5. C-SPAN's own footage
- C-SPAN Copyright & Licensing page (live page blocked automated fetches; Wayback snapshot of 2026-09-17 used): non-commercial posting of C-SPAN's coverage of federal government events is allowed "so long as C-SPAN is attributed as the source". "C-SPAN does NOT permit unlicensed commercial use of any of its audio or video programming (including coverage of federal government events) whether or not C-SPAN is attributed as the source." A licence is required for, among other things, "Any use that relies substantially on C-SPAN content to generate revenue." Simple licences cost $100 per program per year, cover one URL, and are streaming only. [C-SPAN (archived)](http://web.archive.org/web/20260917181332/https://www.c-span.org/about/copyrightsAndLicensing/)
- **For Snortlo:** a monetized channel needs a licence for C-SPAN footage. Floor video from the House or Senate cameras is public domain at its source. C-SPAN's policy restricts streaming or retransmitting coverage "of any event not in the public domain", which implies floor feeds are treated differently. Still, get floor video from house.gov or senate.gov where possible, and don't carry C-SPAN's logo or graphics.

#### 6. US state legislatures
- Works of state governments are **not** covered by 17 USC 105, and the Copyright Office registers state government works. The government-edicts doctrine (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 2020) makes legislators' official work uncopyrightable, but this does not clearly extend to recordings made by legislature staff. "State and local governments may, and often do, claim copyright in their publications." Check every state's terms before using its video. [Copyright Office Compendium ch. 300, §313.6(C)(2)](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap300/ch300-copyrightable-authorship.pdf); [Univ. of Hawaii gov-info FAQ citing CENDI 3.1.3](https://govtinfo.libanswers.com/faq/224247)

### CANADA

#### 7. House of Commons (ParlVU / ourcommons.ca)
- **Speaker's Permission** (ourcommons.ca "Important Notices"; page undated, read 2026-10-08):
  - Rights reserved: "The parliamentary privilege of the House of Commons to control the publication and broadcast of the proceedings … is nonetheless reserved. All copyrights therein are also reserved."
  - What is allowed: "Reproduction … in whole or in part and in any medium, is hereby permitted provided that the reproduction is accurate and is not presented as official."
  - **What is excluded: "This permission does not extend to reproduction, distribution or use for commercial purpose of financial gain."**
  - Enforcement: "Reproduction or use outside this permission or without authorization may be treated as copyright infringement in accordance with the Copyright Act. Authorization may be obtained on written application to the Office of the Speaker."
  - Privilege: permitted reproductions are not protected by parliamentary privilege (this matters for defamation). The notice also says nothing in it reduces the House's privileges.
  - Source: [ourcommons.ca/en/important-notices](https://www.ourcommons.ca/en/important-notices)
- **House of Commons Procedure and Practice, 3rd ed. (2017), ch. 24, footnote 122:** the permission comes from the PROC Committee's Eighth Report, concurred in March 5, 2009. Reproduction is allowed provided it "is accurate, is not presented as official, and is not used for commercial purposes". The book also says the ParlVU webcast "is accessible by other media by permission of the Speaker." [P&P 3rd ed. ch. 24 §2](https://www.ourcommons.ca/procedure/procedure-and-practice-3/ch_24_2-e.html). No newer (4th) edition was found (**UNVERIFIED**).
- **Commercial use:** not allowed without written authorization from the Office of the Speaker.
- **Satire or parody:** the permission doesn't mention it. The "accurate" condition is hard to meet once meme sound effects and captions are added. Fair dealing (see #12) is a separate statutory defence.
- **"Political advertising":** the Speaker's Permission has no clause on it. The "Political Content" rule on the same page governs only the House's own social accounts.
- **How to request:** write to the Office of the Speaker. A media contact (Director of Outreach and Media Relations) is listed on the same page. CPAC also sends requests for Commons footage to House of Commons Broadcasting Services at 613-995-1318 ([CPAC Order a Video](https://www.cpac.ca/order-a-video)). Whether commercial permission is ever granted to entertainment channels is **UNVERIFIED**.

#### 8. Senate of Canada (SenVU / sencanada.ca)
- [sencanada.ca Intellectual Property](https://sencanada.ca/en/intellectual-property/) (undated):
  - What is allowed: reproduction "in whole or in part and in any medium" is permitted "provided that the reproduction is not presented as official and is accurate, that is, the proceedings … are not used in such a way that would compromise the accuracy and integrity of the contents or undermine the dignity of the Senate, its committees or senators."
  - **What is excluded: "This permission does not extend to any use for purposes of financial gain."** Other uses need the Senate's intellectual property request form, otherwise they "may be treated as copyright infringement".
  - Contempt: "The Senate retains the right and privilege to find users in contempt of Parliament if a reproduction or use is not in accordance with this permission."
  - Other content on the site: non-commercial use only; commercial redistribution needs written permission.
  - Social media: Senate social images and videos are "all rights reserved, unless otherwise specified".
  - Senate Emblem: cannot be reproduced for any purpose.
- Copyright questions go to copyright@sen.parl.gc.ca ([CPAC Order a Video](https://www.cpac.ca/order-a-video)). SenVU is not named on the page, but the permission covers proceedings "in any medium".

#### 9. CPAC
- CPAC (Cable Public Affairs Channel Inc.) is a not-for-profit, commercial-free company created in 1992 by a consortium of cable companies that "today includes Rogers, Videotron, Cogeco, EastLink and Access Communications". It is "not affiliated with any government department or agency." [CPAC About](https://www.cpac.ca/about-cpac)
- Terms of Use (posted August 15, 2024): CPAC content "is owned by CPAC, its licensors or others". Viewers get a licence "for your own personal and non-commercial use only", with no modification allowed. Using the content to train AI is banned. [CPAC Terms of Use](https://www.cpac.ca/terms-of-use)
- Order a Video: there is a rate card "for business, commercial or broadcast use of CPAC programming", available from businessaffairs@cpac.ca. Even posting a complete program on a non-commercial site needs a licence. **CPAC excludes House of Commons and Senate proceedings, committees and Supreme Court hearings from its own licensing.** Those go to the House, the Senate or the Court. [CPAC Order a Video](https://www.cpac.ca/order-a-video)
- **For Snortlo:** CPAC footage carries two layers of rights, CPAC's own plus Parliament's. Don't use it.

#### 10. Government of Canada works (canada.ca, pm.gc.ca, department YouTube)
- **Crown copyright:** Copyright Act s.12 says works "prepared or published by or under the direction or control of Her Majesty or any government department" belong to the Crown, for 50 years after first publication. [Copyright Act s.12](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-12.html) (Act current to 2026-09-21)
- **canada.ca Terms and conditions** (Date modified 2025-09-05):
  - Non-commercial reproduction is allowed without charge, with accuracy and attribution.
  - "You may not reproduce materials on this site, in whole or in part, for the purposes of commercial redistribution" without prior written permission.
  - The page does not mention OGL.
  - [canada.ca terms](https://www.canada.ca/en/transparency/terms.html). This page timed out on direct fetch and was read through a summarizing fetch, so wording is paraphrased.
- **Prime Minister's site** ([pm.gc.ca Important Notices](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/important-notices), undated):
  - "Non-commercial reproduction" is allowed with conditions.
  - "Commercial reproduction … is prohibited except with written permission from the Privy Council Office (PCO)" (info@pco-bcp.gc.ca).
  - "Images and videos available through the Prime Minister's X …, YouTube and Facebook accounts are subject to a Canadian Crown Copyright with all rights reserved, unless otherwise specified. We use Creative Commons Licenses to enable sharing … in accordance with the terms set out in the specified Creative Commons license."
  - Practical meaning: a PMO video is usable commercially **only if** it is tagged with a CC licence that allows commercial use (e.g. CC BY). Check each video.
  - Official symbols (Canada Wordmark, Arms of Canada, flag symbol) need prior written authorization for any use.
- **Open Government Licence – Canada, version 2.0** (page modified 2022-12-02):
  - It grants a licence "including for commercial purposes", and you may "modify, publish … in any medium".
  - It covers only "Information … that is offered for use under the terms of this licence". So it applies only to items explicitly released under OGL (mostly open.canada.ca datasets), **not to canada.ca videos by default**.
  - Exemptions: personal information, third-party rights, "names, crests, logos, or other official symbols". It also has a non-endorsement clause.
  - Default attribution: "Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada."
  - [OGL-Canada 2.0](https://open.canada.ca/en/open-government-licence-canada)
  - Whether any federal *video* of politicians is released under OGL is **UNVERIFIED**. Search open.canada.ca for each item.
- **Departments differ:** e.g. National Defence has its own Crown copyright page ([DND Crown copyright](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/corporate/intellectual-property/crown-copyright.html), not reviewed in detail). Treat everything as non-commercial-only unless the specific page or video says otherwise.

#### 11. Provincial legislatures
- **Nova Scotia** ([Legislative TV, Guidelines for Use](https://nslegislature.ca/legislative-business/legislative-tv); [Copyright](https://nslegislature.ca/legal/copyright), © 2025):
  - Permission covers only "educational and research purposes", and broadcasters' "balanced, fair and accurate" news reports.
  - "**The video may only be used with its original audio component and no other audio or video material may be added**."
  - No "political party advertising, election campaigns or any other politically partisan activity".
  - Not "in any edited form that could mislead or misinform".
  - "Any other use … requires the express written approval of the Speaker."
  - Copyright page: non-commercial use only; "Any publication for commercial purposes or financial gain must receive the approval of the Speaker."
  - Contact: LTV@novascotia.ca, 902-424-6420.
  - **Snortlo's meme format directly breaches the "nothing added" rule.**
- **Ontario** ([ola.org Copyright & privacy](https://www.ola.org/en/office-assembly/copyright-privacy), undated; read through a summarizing fetch because the site blocks scripts):
  - The Speaker allows display and reproduction without charge if "The use must be reasonable, fair and non-commercial, and must credit the Assembly".
  - Use is subject to IP law and parliamentary privilege.
  - The coat of arms and trademarks need permission.
  - No video-specific or political-use rule was found.
- **British Columbia** ([Guidelines for Use of Hansard Video](https://leg.bc.ca/parliamentary-business/broadcasts-and-webcasts/guidelines-for-use-of-hansard-video), undated):
  - The Speaker allows replay "for use in schools and for other purposes such as private study, research, criticism, review or newspaper summary", and broadcasters' news use for "fair and accurate reports".
  - "Program material may not be purposefully distorted or used for political party advertising, election campaigns or any other politically partisan activity."
  - "Any other commercial use or rebroadcast of the webcast video requires the express written approval of the Speaker."
  - Note that *criticism/review* is named. Commercial use still needs approval.
- **Québec** ([Assemblée nationale Terms of Use](https://www.assnat.qc.ca/en/propos-site/droits-propriete-intellectuelle.html), last modified April 20, 2009):
  - All content, including videos, is the property of the State (the Assemblée nationale).
  - Free reproduction is allowed if "the use is reasonable, fair, non-commercial and non-profit, the content is not modified or altered, the use does not adversely affect the honour or reputation of the Assemblée nationale or of the persons whose likeness or voice is found" on the site, and the source is credited.
  - Broadcasting debates doesn't need permission, subject to ss.48–49 of the Act respecting the National Assembly (those sections were not reviewed: **UNVERIFIED**).
  - "Content … may not be used for commercial or promotional purposes without prior permission" (renseignements@assnat.qc.ca).
- **Alberta** ([Terms of Use for Audio and Video Recordings](https://assembly.ab.ca/terms-of-use-for-audio-and-video-recordings), undated):
  - Allowed for "personal and public non-commercial use".
  - Recordings "may not be altered or modified, except for length, or used in any form of undignified association".
  - Not in "advertising for political party purposes or during general election or by-election periods".
  - "Use or broadcast … for commercial purposes or financial gain requires the prior written approval of the Speaker" (laocommunications@assembly.ab.ca).
- **Pattern:** every legislature checked is non-commercial only, and most also ban alteration, distortion or "undignified" use. **None is usable for Snortlo without written permission.** Other provinces and territories were not checked.

#### 12. Canadian fair dealing for parody/satire (Copyright Act s.29)
- The statute: "Fair dealing for the purpose of research, private study, education, parody or satire does not infringe copyright." Parody and satire were added in 2012. [s.29](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-29.html). Criticism and review (s.29.1) also qualify but require naming the source, author and broadcaster ([s.29.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-29.1.html)).
- **The non-commercial UGC exception (s.29.21) does not apply.** It requires use "solely for non-commercial purposes". [s.29.21](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-29.21.html)
- **The fairness test** (*CCH v. LSUC*, 2004 SCC 13) weighs six factors: purpose, character, amount, alternatives, nature of the work, and effect on the work. Commercial purpose can make a dealing less fair but doesn't decide the case. [UAlberta summary quoting CCH paras 53–60](https://www.ualberta.ca/copyright/intro-to-copyright-law/fair-dealing/factors)
- **What "parody" means in Canadian law:** in the main case so far (*United Airlines v. Cooperstock*, 2017 FC 616), parody has two parts: "the evocation of an existing work while exhibiting noticeable differences and the expression of mockery or humour". The defence still **failed** because the dealing was unfair: the use was mean-spirited and confusing. [Bennett Jones summary](https://www.bennettjones.com/UnitedAirlinesGroundsComplaintWebsite)
- **How this might apply to Snortlo meme edits:**
  - Short clips with added sound effects and captions that mock a politician's public conduct fit the *purpose* (humour or mockery about the clip).
  - Short excerpts help the *amount* factor.
  - Parliament doesn't sell the footage commercially, which helps the *effect* factor (arguable).
  - Public proceedings meant for wide circulation help the *nature* factor.
  - Monetization and a channel built entirely on other people's footage count against fairness.
  - Edits that mislead about what happened count against fairness. They also breach the "accurate" and "not distorted" conditions and create defamation risk.
- **What fair dealing does NOT fix:**
  1. Parliamentary privilege. Both Houses reserve the right to control broadcast and to find users in contempt. Contempt against a satirist has never been used as far as found (**UNVERIFIED**), but the risk is not zero.
  2. Moral rights (s.28.2) cover distortion that harms the author's honour, though they are unlikely to be asserted for Hansard video. [s.28.2](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-28.2.html)
  3. Platform takedowns. YouTube and Meta act on complaints first.
  4. Defamation or appropriation of personality (not researched: **UNVERIFIED**).
- **Practical risk:** copyright litigation by a legislature against a meme channel seems unlikely, but no case law was found on monetized parody of parliamentary video (**UNVERIFIED**). The realistic risks are takedown or copyright-strike requests, demonetization, and reputational or contempt exposure. Fair dealing is a fallback, not a business model. **Recommendation: no Canadian legislature footage until written permission is in hand.** If the Speaker's offices are asked, describe the format honestly.

#### 13. YouTube
- **Channel monetization policies** ([YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)):
  - July 15, 2025 update: "repetitious content" was renamed "**inauthentic content**" to cover "repetitive or mass-produced" content. The **reused content** policy (which "reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos") did not change.
  - Reused content means repurposing other content "without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value". It "applies to your channel as a whole". It is "not based on copyright, permission, or fair use", so **public-domain footage can still fail it**.
  - Allowed: "Using clips for a critical review", "Edited footage with audio and visual effects on top of the video's reused content that demonstrates substantive editing and shows it's unique to your channel".
  - Not allowed: "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative", "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications", and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production".
  - The newer "**Unsatisfying or off-putting content**" section also targets formulaic, interchangeable videos.
  - **For Snortlo:** each clip needs a visible creative layer: its own captions, timing, sound design and a point of view. Avoid templated sameness across uploads.
- **Misleading or manipulated media** ([Misinformation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10834785?hl=en); [Elections misinformation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10835034?hl=en)):
  - Banned: content "technically manipulated or doctored in a way that misleads users (usually beyond clips taken out of context) and may pose a serious risk of egregious harm". Example: footage altered "to make a candidate for public office falsely claim they're dropping out".
  - Exceptions can apply for added context and for "congressional or parliamentary proceedings".
- **AI or altered-content disclosure** ([Disclosing altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)):
  - Disclosure is required if content "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do" or "Alters footage of a real event".
  - Minor or aesthetic edits and clearly unrealistic effects don't need disclosure.
  - Snortlo should not use AI voices or face-swaps at all (house rule).
- **Advertiser-friendly guidelines** ([AFG](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)):
  - Political content is not its own limited-ads category.
  - The relevant risks: **"Incendiary and demeaning"**, where "Content that focuses on shaming or insulting an individual or group" gets limited or no ads.
  - Also relevant: "Inappropriate language" (profanity in captions or titles) and "Harmful misinformation" ("demonstrably false information about … election results …").
  - Keep the humour at the "absurd moment" level, not insults. Keep titles and thumbnails clean.

## (c) House rules for Snortlo politics clips

**Source rules**
1. **Use only approved sources** (status "Yes" in the table): US House or Senate floor video taken from house.gov or senate.gov feeds, committee-produced video, and government-made White House, NASA, DVIDS and NOAA footage. Canadian or provincial legislature footage is used **only with written Speaker permission on file**. Federal Canadian video is used only if it is tagged OGL or CC BY.
2. **Never use C-SPAN, CPAC or any network footage** without a licence. Never rip from news uploads. Remove bugs and chyrons, or better, start from the raw government feed.
3. **Keep a source log for every clip:** original URL, date, timestamps, and why it is clear. Save the government feed link.

**Accuracy rules**
4. **One continuous clip per moment.** No splicing words or sentences to change meaning. No reordering. No speeding up or slowing down speech to mislead. Trimming length is fine.
5. **Context line on screen:** chamber, date and topic (e.g. "US Senate floor · 2026-03-04 · debate on X"). Don't caption a clip as saying something it doesn't.

**No synthetic content**
6. **No AI voices, deepfakes, face or lip edits, or invented quotes.** Never make a real person appear to say or do something they didn't. This covers YouTube's AI-disclosure triggers and the misinformation rules.
7. **Sound effects and captions are clearly comedic overlays**, never mixed to sound like the speaker's own audio. Captions read as jokes, not quotes.

**No endorsement**
8. **No implied endorsement.** Don't use the presidential, House or Senate seals, the NASA meatball or military insignia as branding or in thumbnails. Never put government footage in ads, sponsor reads or product promos. Add "Not affiliated with or endorsed by any government" in the description. For DVIDS footage, include the DoW non-endorsement line.
9. **No campaign use and no election-period targeting.** Never post anything that tells people how to vote. Never coordinate with any party or candidate. Never boost political clips.

**Fairness and tone**
10. **Balance across parties.** Track a running tally and aim for rough parity of who gets roasted, so the channel reads as satire of politics, not partisan content.
11. **Punch at moments, not people.** Mock the absurd moment (a gaffe, a pause, a mic mishap), not anyone's identity, health or family. No slurs or profanity in titles or thumbnails, which keeps the advertiser-friendly guidelines happy.

**Credit and third-party material**
12. **Credit the source on screen and in the description,** e.g. "Footage: U.S. House of Representatives (public domain)". Add "CC BY 3.0 + credit" for White House third-party items. For any Canadian footage used under permission, add a "Reproduced with permission of the Speaker" line if the permission requires it.
13. **Remove third-party copyrighted material:** news chyrons, network logos, background music, played-in videos, and exhibits held up in hearings. Use only licensed or own-made sound effects and music.

**Originality and ops**
14. **Add real originality to every upload:** own captions, edit timing, sound design and a short take. Vary formats, because templated sameness triggers YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule and Meta's "unoriginal" rule.
15. **If a takedown or claim arrives**, stop and document. Dispute only public-domain US floor or committee footage, with the source log as evidence. Never dispute claims on Canadian footage without permission on file.
